Local Ground & What It Means for Your Project

Doolittle sits on the Ozark plateau, and the ground shows it. Under the topsoil you often hit chert and rock, and the terrain rolls between the highway grade and the surrounding ridges and draws. That rocky, uneven ground is exactly why the right equipment matters — thin soil over rock changes how a footing gets dug, how a driveway is based, and how water moves across a lot after a hard rain.

Being tucked between I-44 and old Route 66 also means a lot of local drives and yards feed toward the highway ditches and low spots, so grading and drainage go hand in hand out here. And with the Rolla-area airports and the Fort Wood approach nearby, plenty of Doolittle parcels are open acreage that needs clearing or shaping before anything else can happen. We plan the dirt work around that reality instead of fighting it.

  • Rock and chert soils — common on the plateau; we dig, base and backfill with that in mind so driveways and slabs hold up.
  • Rolling terrain and draws — grading and retaining work keep water and vehicles moving where the ground isn't flat.
  • Long rural driveways — acreage off the county roads often needs proper base, crowning and culverts, not just a load of gravel.
  • Highway-adjacent lots — drainage toward the Route 66 / I-44 ditches has to be graded so runoff leaves your property, not pools on it.

What Affects the Cost

Every Doolittle project is priced from the ground in front of us, never a guess over the phone. The things that move the number are the practical ones:

  • Size and scope — square footage of concrete, length of driveway, or acres to clear.
  • Access — how easily equipment can reach the work on a rural or highway-side lot.
  • Soil and rock — how much chert or rock we hit affects digging and base prep.
  • Distance and haul — how far material and equipment have to travel (short, from our nearby base).
  • Materials — the type and amount of gravel, concrete, pipe or fill the job calls for.
