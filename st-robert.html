The St. Robert & Fort Wood Ground We Work

St. Robert climbs the Ozark plateau on the north side of Fort Leonard Wood, and the ground here shows it. Building lots and yards run over chert and rock-shot clay, with grades that roll rather than sit flat. On new construction that means a builder or homeowner needs a properly cut and compacted pad, positive fall away from the foundation, and a plan for where storm water goes before the first footing is poured. On the commercial side, near the exit 161 interchange and the Missouri Avenue business strip, sites need real base prep and drainage to hold up under traffic and parking.

We know this corner of Pulaski County because we work it — from the newer residential streets spreading north and east of town, to acreage on the edges where families want a home site cleared and a long driveway put in. Whether you're a military family buying your first place, a landlord adding units, or a builder prepping a lot, the dirt and rock underneath dictate the approach, and we plan for it up front.

St. Robert Site Work & Concrete FAQs

Do you work in St. Robert and around Fort Leonard Wood?

Yes. St. Robert is a regular part of our service area — it's a straight shot west down I-44 from our St. James base, roughly 45 minutes. We serve the St. Robert and Fort Wood area along with nearby Waynesville and the towns back up the interstate toward Rolla.

Can you handle site work for new construction in a St. Robert subdivision?

That's a lot of what we do here. As the town keeps growing, builders and homeowners need lots cleared, building pads cut and compacted, footings dug, utilities trenched and driveways graded. We can take a raw lot all the way through to a finished concrete driveway or slab with one crew.

Will the rock and clay on my lot be a problem?

The Ozark plateau around St. Robert is full of chert and rock-shot clay, and it does affect digging and grading. It's not a problem so much as something to plan for — we bring machines sized for it and account for the ground when we scope the work and give you an estimate.

My yard or driveway floods after a hard rain — can you fix that?

Usually, yes. On the rolling lots around St. Robert, water has to be routed on purpose. We install swales, culverts and French drains to move water away from your foundation, yard and driveway. We'll look at where the water comes from and where it needs to go before we dig.

Do you pour the concrete yourselves or hire it out?

We pour it ourselves. Concrete is done in-house — driveways, patios, sidewalks, shop and garage slabs, and footings — which matters when St. Robert construction is running on a tight schedule and you don't want to wait on an outside crew.
