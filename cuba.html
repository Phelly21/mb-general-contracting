Cuba & the Crawford County Ground We Work

Cuba is best known for its outdoor murals and the restored Wagon Wheel Motel along historic Route 66, and the town still hugs that old highway corridor where I-44 now carries the traffic. Away from the downtown grid, the area quickly turns to farm-and-wine country — pasture, cropland, small farms and vineyards spread across the rolling Ozark-border landscape north and south of the interstate. That mix shapes the work: an in-town lot might need a clean concrete pour or a drainage fix, while an acreage parcel needs a long driveway cut in, brush and trees cleared, and water routed away from a house or barn.

The ground out here is typical of the region — rolling terrain with clay and rock that varies from one parcel to the next, and enough slope in places that grading and drainage have to be planned, not guessed at. Rural properties often sit well off the county road, so access, driveway length and where the water wants to go are the things that drive a site plan. We read the lay of the land first, then build to it.

Cuba Area FAQs

Do you actually cover Cuba, or just St. James and Rolla?

We cover Cuba. It's just east of St. James at I-44 exit 208 — an easy run up the interstate for us — so Crawford County has always been part of our service area alongside St. James, Rolla and Phelps County.

Can you put in a driveway on rural acreage outside town?

Yes. A lot of our Cuba-area work is long gravel driveways and rural approaches off county roads, out in the farm-and-wine country. We cut in new drives, build them up so they drain, and regrade existing drives that have rutted or washboarded. See our driveway grading page for more.

My yard or field holds water after it rains — can you fix that?

Usually, yes. On the rolling ground around Cuba, standing water is almost always a grading and drainage problem. We use regrading, swales, culverts and French drains to move water off lots and away from houses, barns and low farm ground. Here's our drainage page.

Do you clear brush and trees to open up land near Cuba?

Yes. We clear brush, trees and overgrowth to open up pasture, building sites and fence lines, and we run a forestry mulcher for fast, clean work on wooded acreage. See land clearing for details.

Do you pour the concrete yourself or hire it out?

We pour it ourselves, in-house — driveways, slabs, patios, sidewalks and shop floors. That means the same crew handles the excavating and site prep and the finished concrete, so nothing falls through the cracks between trades.

How do I get an estimate for a Cuba project?

Call or text (573) 728-2550, or send your details through our contact page. We'll set up a free, in-person visit to look at the ground in Cuba, then walk you through the work. Being right down I-44, we can usually get out to you without much of a wait.

What affects the price of a job out here?

Cost depends on the specifics of your property — the size of the job, how much access there is, soil and rock conditions, how far material has to travel, and the materials themselves. That's why we look at the actual site first rather than quoting blind.
