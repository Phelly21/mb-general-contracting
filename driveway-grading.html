Out here, most driveways are gravel — and a gravel driveway is only as good as the base under it and the drainage around it. Plenty of drives get "fixed" by dumping a load of rock on top of soft, rutted ground. That looks better for a month, then the rock disappears into the mud and the potholes come right back. We do it differently. Whether you need a brand-new driveway cut in from the county road or a worn-out lane brought back to life, we grade the ground, build a stable base, shape a crown that sheds water, and top it with the right rock so it actually lasts.

What We Do — Driveway Services

  • New gravel driveway installation — we grub off topsoil and roots, cut the driveway to grade, compact a stone base and top it with surface rock so you start with a solid, stable drive instead of soft dirt.
  • Driveway regrading & repair — we blade out ruts, potholes and washboarding, reclaim rock that has sunk into the ground, and re-crown the surface so it drains instead of collecting water.

  • Culvert & ditch install — we size and set culvert pipe at the road entrance and low spots, and cut ditching so road and driveway water keeps moving where it should.
  • Widening, turnarounds & parking pads — add a second lane, a wider entrance, a turnaround so you're not backing onto the road, or a graveled parking pad for extra vehicles, trailers or equipment.
  • Long rural & farm lanes — access drives back to homes, shops, barns, hunting cabins and fields, built to carry delivery trucks, propane trucks and farm equipment.
  • Base prep for concrete or asphalt — if you're going hard-surface, we grade and compact a proper base so your concrete driveway or asphalt has solid support under it.

Drainage Is Everything

A driveway that holds water is a driveway that fails. The crown, the ditching and the culverts are what separate a drive that lasts years from one that washes out every spring. We build in real drainage from the start — and if your property has bigger water problems, we can address those with the same equipment while we're on site.

Built for Phelps County Ground & Weather

Driveways in this part of the Ozarks face a specific set of challenges, and building for them is the whole job. The ground around Rolla and St. James is hilly Ozark-plateau terrain with plenty of chert and rock, sloping lots, and creek bottoms that hold moisture. When a heavy Missouri rain comes through, water finds the low path fast — and if that path is your driveway, it will cut ruts and carry your rock down the hill.

That's why crown and drainage matter so much here. On a sloped lot, we shape the drive so water sheds off the sides into ditches instead of running the length of it. Where a driveway crosses a ditch line at the road, a properly sized culvert keeps that flow moving underneath instead of over the top. On long rural drives out toward Doolittle, Newburg and the country around St. James, we build for the reality that these lanes carry everything from the family truck to the UPS driver to a loaded propane or gravel truck — and they need to stay passable in mud season, not just in July. We match the base and rock to your actual soil, because clay bottoms, rocky sidehills and soft creek-bottom ground each need a different approach.

Signs Your Driveway Needs Work

You Might Just Need Regrading

If your drive is washboarded, holds puddles after rain, has ruts you feel in the steering wheel, or has lost so much rock that dirt shows through, a proper regrade and re-crown usually brings it back — often without a full rebuild.

You Might Need a Rebuild

If rock keeps vanishing no matter how much you add, the driveway turns to mud every spring, or it washes out in the same spot every year, the base and drainage are the real problem — and fixing those is what makes the repair stick.

  • Standing water or soft spots — a sign the crown is gone and water is sitting on the surface instead of running off.
  • Washouts and gullies — usually a drainage and culvert issue, not just a rock issue.
  • Potholes and washboarding — the surface has broken down and needs blading and fresh rock.
  • Rock disappearing into mud — the base is failing and rock is being pushed into soft ground below.
  • No turnaround — if you're backing onto a county road or the highway, a turnaround or wider pad makes it safer.

What Affects the Cost of a Driveway

Every driveway is different, so we give real numbers only after seeing the site. These are the main things that move the price up or down:

  • Length and width — a short residential drive takes far less material and time than a long rural lane or a widened, multi-vehicle entrance.
  • Site access & slope — steep, wooded or tight lots take more work to grade and shape than open, level ground.
  • Ground & soil conditions — soft clay or creek-bottom ground needs more base than firm, rocky sidehill.
  • Amount and type of rock — the depth of base and surface rock needed for your traffic and soil.
  • Drainage work — how much ditching is required and how many culverts need to be sized and set.
  • Clearing & prep — whether trees, brush or old surface need to be removed before we can build.
  • Distance and haul — how far material has to be trucked to your property.

Driveway FAQs

How much gravel does a driveway need?

It depends on your soil and how much traffic the drive carries, but a durable gravel driveway generally needs a compacted base of larger crushed rock with several inches of surface rock on top — more depth for long drives, soft ground or heavy vehicles. We measure and spec the right depth for your ground during the free on-site estimate.

My driveway washes out every spring — can you fix that?

Yes, and it's one of the most common jobs we do. A drive that washes out almost always has a crown and drainage problem: water is running down the surface instead of off the sides. We re-shape the driveway, build the correct crown, cut ditching and set or resize culverts so water leaves the drive instead of cutting it apart.

Do you install culverts?

We do. We size the culvert pipe for the amount of water it has to carry, set it at the correct depth and grade at your road entrance or across low spots, and cut the ditching around it so both road and driveway water flow the way they should.

Can you just add rock to my existing driveway?

Sometimes fresh surface rock and a good blading is all a drive needs. But if rock keeps disappearing into the mud, the base or drainage has failed, and more rock alone won't hold. We'll tell you honestly at the estimate whether a regrade will do it or whether you'll get more value from a proper rebuild.

Should I put in a gravel driveway or concrete?

Both have their place. Gravel costs less up front, is easy to repair, and suits long rural lanes and farm access. Concrete costs more but gives you a clean, low-maintenance, mud-free surface that's great for shorter drives, parking areas and anywhere you want a finished look. Because we build gravel drives and pour concrete in-house, we can walk you through the trade-offs without steering you either way.

Can you build a base for a concrete or asphalt driveway?

Yes. A hard surface is only as good as the base under it. We grade and compact a proper stone base with the right drainage so your concrete or asphalt has solid, stable support and doesn't crack or settle from a weak foundation.

Do you build long rural and farm driveways?

Regularly. We cut and build access lanes running back to homes, shops, barns, cabins and fields around St. James, Newburg and the country toward Doolittle and beyond — sized to carry farm equipment, delivery and propane trucks, and to stay passable through mud season.

How long does a driveway job take?

A straightforward regrade can often be done in a day, while a new drive, a long lane, or a job with significant drainage and culvert work takes longer. We'll give you a realistic timeframe with your estimate once we've seen the site.
