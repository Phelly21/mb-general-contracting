Excavation Services We Provide

We take on excavating work of almost any size, from a single-day skid-steer job in a tight backyard to multi-day site prep and pond digs on open acreage. The core services we're called for most often across Phelps County:

  • Site preparation & building pads — clearing, stripping topsoil, cutting and filling, and compacting solid pads for homes, shops, garages, barns, pole buildings and additions so the structure sits level and drains away from the walls.
  • Basement & footing excavation — dug to your plan and to the correct depth for new construction, with clean, square walls and a bottom your concrete crew can work from.
  • Pond digs, cleanouts, dams & spillways — new farm and recreational ponds, plus cleaning out, deepening or expanding existing ones, including rebuilding the dam and shaping a proper spillway that won't wash out.
  • Demolition & debris removal — tearing down old houses, mobile homes, barns, sheds and failed foundations, then breaking up and hauling off the concrete, block and debris so you're left with clean ground.
  • Earthmoving & hauling — moving dirt, rock and fill on-site, plus bringing in fill material or hauling excess spoil away when a job leaves you with more than you can use.
  • Rough & finish grading — shaping lots, pads and yards so water runs where it should, then finishing the surface tight and true for seeding, gravel or slab.
  • Trenching — for footings, utilities, electrical, drainage lines and waterlines, dug to depth and backfilled correctly. See our waterline & utility work for line installs.
  • Land clearing & grubbing — brush, trees and root balls removed and the ground grubbed clean before digging begins; often paired with our land & brush clearing service.

Digging in Phelps County & the Ozark Plateau

Excavating in this part of Missouri is not the same as digging in flat, deep-soil farm country. We're on the northern edge of the Ozark Plateau, where the topsoil is often thin and chert gravel, sandstone and dolomite rock sit close to the surface. A dig that looks simple from the driveway can hit hard rock two feet down, and a good excavator has to read the ground and adjust rather than force it. This is exactly the kind of rock we work in every week, which is why local landowners call us instead of a company hauling equipment down from the city that has never touched Ozark chert.

The terrain matters just as much. Around St. James, our home base, the ground rolls between small farms, wooded acreage and vineyard country near the Meramec headwaters — plenty of building pads and pond sites, but also plenty of slope that has to be cut and shaped so a structure sits level and water drains away. Over in Rolla, the county seat and largest town in the area, we work both older in-town lots and newer subdivisions pushing out onto hilly chert ground where footing excavation and grading take real know-how. And in rural spots like Newburg, down on the Little Piney with its creek bottoms, long driveways and steep slopes, site prep and pond work often come with access challenges that a local crew plans for from the start.

Weather is part of the plan too. Our clay-and-chert soils hold water and turn greasy after a hard Missouri rain, and spring can string wet days together. We time excavation and hauling around the ground conditions so we're not rutting up your property or compacting mud that will never drain right. Rural properties also bring their own realities — septic fields, wells, buried lines, gravel lanes and long distances from the road — and we account for all of it before the first bucket of dirt comes out.

Signs It's Time to Call an Excavator

Sometimes the need is obvious — you're building and you need a hole. Other times the ground is telling you something. Common situations we're called out for:

You're About to Build

A new home, shop, garage, barn or addition all start with a properly dug and compacted pad, footings or basement. Getting this right is what keeps the structure level and the concrete from cracking.

Water Is Going the Wrong Way

If runoff pools against a foundation, floods a low spot or washes out a lane, the fix usually starts with re-grading and often ties into drainage work to move water away for good.

You Want a Pond — or Yours Is Silted In

Dry old ponds, ponds full of muck and cattails, or a bare spot where you've always wanted water. We dig new ponds and clean out and rebuild tired ones, dam and spillway included.

Something Needs to Come Down

An old house, mobile home, barn or crumbling foundation taking up space or posing a hazard. We demolish it, break out the concrete, and haul the debris off so you're left with usable ground.

What Affects the Cost of Excavating

Every site is different, so there's no flat price for excavation — the honest answer is that cost depends on the job in front of us. When we come out for your free estimate, these are the factors we're weighing:

  • Size and depth of the dig — the volume of dirt to be moved and how deep we have to go are the biggest drivers.
  • Soil and rock conditions — sandy topsoil digs fast; solid chert, sandstone or ledge rock takes more time, heavier equipment and sometimes ripping or breaking.
  • Site access — an open field is easy; a tight backyard, a long steep driveway or working around a house, well and septic all add care and time.
  • Haul distance — whether spoil can be spread on-site or has to be trucked off, and how far fill material has to come in.
  • Materials brought in — fill dirt, gravel, rock or pipe needed to complete the job.
  • Grading and finish required — a rough cut costs less than a tight finish grade ready for slab or seed.
  • Cleanup and disposal — demolition debris, concrete, stumps and old material that has to be hauled and disposed of.

We'll always explain what's driving the number so you understand exactly what you're paying for. No surprises added later.

Excavating FAQs

Do you handle small residential jobs or just big projects?

Both. We size the equipment to the job — a mini excavator and skid steer for tight backyard work or a single trench, or dozers and full-size excavators for site pads and pond digs. No job is too small to call about.

Can you dig a pond on my property?

Yes. We build new farm and recreational ponds and clean out, deepen or expand existing ones, including rebuilding the dam and shaping a spillway that won't wash out. We'll walk the site with you to find the best location, size and depth for how the ground and water sit on your place.

What if you hit rock while digging?

Around here, we usually expect it. Phelps County ground often has chert and rock close to the surface, so we plan for it up front and bring equipment that can handle it. If a dig turns out to be far more rock than anyone could see from the surface, we'll stop and talk it through with you before doing anything that changes the estimate.

Do you demolish and remove old buildings and foundations?

Yes. We tear down old houses, mobile homes, barns and sheds, break out failed concrete and foundations, and haul off the debris so you're left with clean, usable ground ready for whatever comes next.

Will you work around my well, septic and buried lines?

Absolutely — it's routine on rural properties. We locate and account for wells, septic fields, tanks and any buried lines before we dig, and work carefully around them so nothing gets damaged.

How soon can you start?

It depends on the season and how much weather we've had, since wet ground can push jobs back a few days. Give us a call or text and we'll give you an honest timeline for your project and get you on the schedule.
