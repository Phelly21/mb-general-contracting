A Local Contractor for Waynesville Property Owners

Waynesville is the seat of Pulaski County and one of the older towns on this stretch of Route 66, home to the historic Old Stagecoach Stop downtown and built along the Roubidoux Creek and its spring. MB General Contracting is based just up the I-44 corridor in St. James, so the Waynesville area is a short, familiar drive for us — close enough to come out, walk your property in person, and give you a free estimate without charging you for the trip.

We work for homeowners, landowners, farmers and builders throughout the area, from in-town lots near downtown Waynesville to acreage tracts and long private drives out in the hills. Because so much of Pulaski County sits on slope, near a creek, or backs up to timber, the projects we see most here revolve around moving water, holding back grade, and getting solid access in and out — exactly the site work an owner-operated crew with dozers, excavators and a forestry mulcher is built for.

Waynesville Terrain: Bluffs, Creek Bottoms & Ozark Slope

The ground around Waynesville is genuine Ozark plateau — rolling to steep, with rock bluffs, chert and shallow bedrock on the ridges and rich, low bottoms along the Roubidoux and its feeder branches. That combination is beautiful to live on and tricky to build on. Water runs fast off the hillsides and collects in the low spots, and rock can turn up a foot down when you least expect it. On the flip side, the creek bottoms near the Roubidoux and the Big Piney sit low enough that drainage and proper grade become the whole ballgame.

Waynesville also sits right next to Fort Leonard Wood, and along with neighboring St. Robert the area keeps growing with new homes and military families settling in. Whether you're prepping a fresh building pad on a hillside lot, cutting a driveway up a grade, or fixing a yard that floods every spring, we plan the work around how water and rock actually behave on your specific piece of ground.

Waynesville Site Work FAQs

Do you travel out to Waynesville from St. James?

Yes. Waynesville and Pulaski County are a regular part of our service area — a short drive west along the I-44 corridor from our St. James base. We come out for a free, in-person estimate and handle projects across the Waynesville and St. Robert area.

My lot is steep and rocky — can you still build a pad or driveway?

That's the norm around Waynesville, and it's what our equipment is made for. We use dozers and excavators to cut into hillside and rocky ground, then set a stable, level base for a building pad, driveway or slab. On slopes we'll also talk through whether a retaining wall makes the space more usable.

Water pools in my yard every spring — what can you do?

On Waynesville's terrain, water coming off the hills or backing up near the Roubidoux has to be given somewhere to go. We look at your grade and install the right mix of regrading, swales, culverts or French drains so runoff moves away from your home and driveway instead of sitting in the low spots. See our drainage page for how we approach it.

My gravel driveway keeps washing out on the grade. Can you fix it?

Yes — washouts on Ozark grades usually come down to crown, rock and drainage. We reshape the drive with the right crown, add and compact proper base rock, and cut ditches or culverts so water sheds off instead of running down the middle. More on our driveway grading page.

Do you clear wooded acreage around Pulaski County?

We do. A lot of the ground around Waynesville is timber and brush. Our forestry mulcher and equipment open up building sites, pastures, trails, fencerows and long driveways. Details on the land clearing page.

Do you pour concrete yourself or hire it out?

We pour it ourselves. MB does concrete in-house — driveways, slabs, footings, sidewalks and patios — so the same crew that preps the ground also handles the flatwork, with the base prep this soil needs. See the concrete page for more.
